# The Gateaux Cats - Character Guide

## Overview
The Gateaux café is staffed by five distinct cats, each representing different aspects of language learning. They range from proper and professional to street-smart and sassy, helping players learn both formal phrases and playful slang.

---

## 1. Amélie (The Elegant Mentor)
**Role:** Teaches formal greetings and polite conversation  
**Language Focus:** Proper French & Spanish basics  
**Appearance:** Silver Persian cat with a pearl collar, always impeccably groomed  
**Personality:** 
- Refined and patient, like a finishing school teacher
- Speaks with perfect pronunciation and grammar
- Occasionally lets slip that she wasn't always so posh
- Has a secret fondness for street food

**Sample Phrases:**
- "Bonjour, comment allez-vous?" (Hello, how are you?)
- "Buenos días, ¿cómo está usted?" (Good morning, how are you?)

---

## 2. Marcel (The Street-Smart Regular)
**Role:** Teaches casual greetings and everyday slang  
**Language Focus:** Street French & Spanish slang  
**Appearance:** Orange tabby with a slightly crooked ear, wears a backwards cap  
**Personality:**
- Former alley cat turned café regular
- Knows all the local gossip and shortcuts
- Friendly rivalry with Amélie about "proper" language
- Master of witty comebacks and playful teasing

**Sample Phrases:**
- "Salut mec, ça roule?" (Hey dude, how's it rolling?)
- "¿Qué onda, compa?" (What's up, buddy?)

---

## 3. Café (The Overwhelmed Barista)
**Role:** Teaches coffee orders and food vocabulary  
**Language Focus:** Café culture and food slang  
**Appearance:** Siamese cat with coffee-stained apron, always slightly frazzled  
**Personality:**
- Perpetually caffeinated and multitasking
- Mutters sarcastic comments under their breath
- Actually loves their job despite constant complaining
- Has memorized everyone's "usual" order

**Sample Phrases:**
- "Un autre double espresso? T'es sérieux?" (Another double espresso? Are you serious?)
- "¿En serio quieres más azúcar?" (Seriously, you want more sugar?)

---

## 4. Bisou (The Hopeless Romantic)
**Role:** Teaches flirting phrases and compliments  
**Language Focus:** Romance and social phrases (PG-13)  
**Appearance:** Fluffy white cat with pink bow, dramatic eyelashes  
**Personality:**
- Lives for drama and matchmaking
- Gives unsolicited relationship advice
- Master of the dramatic sigh and eye roll
- Secretly writes terrible poetry

**Sample Phrases:**
- "Tu as de beaux yeux... pour un touriste" (You have beautiful eyes... for a tourist)
- "¿Vienes aquí seguido, o solo cuando quieres romper corazones?" (Do you come here often, or only when you want to break hearts?)

---

## 5. Gaston (The Grumpy Food Critic)
**Role:** Teaches complaints and opinions  
**Language Focus:** Expressing dissatisfaction with humor  
**Appearance:** British Shorthair, perpetually unimpressed expression  
**Personality:**
- Former food critic, now judges everyone's taste
- Secretly has a heart of gold
- His insults are actually signs of affection
- Impossible to please, but tries to help anyway

**Sample Phrases:**
- "Ce croissant est plus sec que mon sens de l'humour" (This croissant is drier than my sense of humor)
- "¿Llamas a esto café? Mi abuela hace mejor café, y ella está muerta" (You call this coffee? My grandma makes better coffee, and she's dead)

---

## Character Interactions

### Amélie & Marcel
These two represent the eternal struggle between "proper" language and how people actually talk. Their banter teaches players when to use formal vs. informal speech.

### Café & Gaston
The overworked barista and impossible-to-please critic create comedy gold while teaching food vocabulary and how to express opinions politely (or not so politely).

### Bisou & Everyone
Bisou tries to create romantic tension with everyone, leading to humorous rejections and teaching players how to navigate social situations with grace and humor.

---

## Language Learning Philosophy

Each cat represents a different truth about language learning:
- **Amélie:** You need the basics to be understood
- **Marcel:** Real people don't talk like textbooks
- **Café:** Context and tone matter as much as words
- **Bisou:** Language is about connection (even failed connection)
- **Gaston:** Humor transcends language barriers

Together, they create a complete picture of how language actually works in the real world - messy, funny, and wonderfully human (or feline).
